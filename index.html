Bring your own model

The endpoint is a plain text field, and the API key is optional. That means the obvious providers — OpenRouter, Groq, Together, DeepInfra, Nous Portal — and it equally means a model running on your own computer, reached from anywhere over your own private network.

Free models are badged so you know before you send, and your choice is remembered per conversation. Nothing is metered by us, because we are not in the path: you pay your provider, or nobody.

Or your own agent

A chat client answers questions. Hermes, the open-source agent from Nous Research, does work — it searches, reads, runs commands, remembers, and touches your actual files. Kite shows every step as it happens and tells you when a long task is finished.

Run it on your Mac or on a server; Kite connects to either. That is the thing no other client on your phone can offer, and it is why Kite exists.

Connecting is the whole point

Every other way of doing this asks you to set up a tunnel, a certificate, or a hostname first. Kite asks you to scan a code. It finds your computer on your network, and away from home it uses your own private network — so your agent is never exposed to the internet.

What it does

  • See what it’s doing. Searching, reading, running commands — the steps appear as they happen. Tap any step to see the command it actually ran.
  • Ask without opening it. “Ask Kite” and the answer comes back without the app opening. “Send to Kite” hands over a long job and notifies you when it lands. Both work from Shortcuts, an automation or the Action button.
  • Know when it’s done. A notification when a long task finishes, and a live view on your Lock Screen while it works.
  • Send it anything. Share a link, a photo or a document from any app straight to your agent — PDFs are read on your phone, including scans. Photos need a model that can see images; pick one and your agent reads them too.
  • Pick up where you left off. Conversations are shared with your desktop, your terminal and anything else that talks to your agent.

More than one machine

A Mac at home and a server that never sleeps: Kite keeps both and switches with a tap, each remembering its own model. Adding a server takes no code and no terminal — type its name on your tailnet and it hands the phone what it needs.

Your data stays yours

Your conversations go between your phone and your own computer. None of them passes through us, and there is no account, no analytics and no tracking. The only thing we ever receive is a notification token, so a finished task can buzz your phone — and notifications carry no message content. The privacy policy says exactly what that means.

Notifications carry no message content, only the fact that a reply arrived. See the privacy policy, which is short because there is little to describe.

No agent yet?

Kite also connects straight to a model — OpenRouter or Nous Portal — using your own API key, with no Hermes agent involved at all. You get a plain chat with no memory, tools or access to your files, which is a different thing from an agent, but it works on its own and it works anywhere. Many of the models are free.

You can switch between the two at any time. Your agent and your direct connection are kept separate, so setting one up never disturbs the other.

What you need

  • An iPhone or iPad.
  • Somewhere to send your messages. Any one of: an API key for a hosted provider — OpenRouter, Groq, Together, Nous Portal; a model running on your own computer via Ollama or LM Studio, which needs no key at all; or the Hermes agent, if you want tools, memory and your files.
  • Only for an agent: Kite Helper on the machine it runs on, so your phone never holds the agent's key.
  • Tailscale on your phone and that machine — needed to reach anything at home from outside, and always needed for a server. Not needed for a hosted provider.
